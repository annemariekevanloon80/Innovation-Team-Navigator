Welcome to the Innovation Team approach

Innovation Teams bring educational professionals together to explore complex challenges, connect research with professional knowledge, develop practical tools and learn from testing them. The full method includes eight sessions.

In the EAPRIL workshop: Session 1 is deliberately brief. In a full Innovation Team, participants first get to know each other, share their expertise and networks, establish ways of working and explore the meanings behind their challenge. Today you can work individually and continue later.
In the workshop, aim to experience the approach rather than complete every activity. You can revisit and revise any session.

Choose your innovation challenge

Select one of three AI-in-education examples or introduce your own challenge. The suggested sources are starting points—not ready-made answers.

1. Research knowledge

Read an abstract from a suggested paper or find relevant research yourself. Record what you learned and why it matters to your challenge.

2. Professional and expert knowledge

Ask another participant, draw on experience in your context, or decide which expert or stakeholder you would consult.

3. Connect research and practice

Where do the research findings and professional perspectives support, complement or challenge one another?

Map your insights

Make a simple mind map around your challenge. Use short keywords and phrases. Add what you learned, what you want to ask experts, and what is still missing.

Your innovation challenge

Define your focus

What is the core of your challenge? For whom does it matter? Narrow your scope before thinking about solutions.

Keep the question open enough to explore several possible tools in Session 4. A provisional question is sufficient for the conference workshop.

Define design criteria

What should a future tool contribute or make possible? Formulate general requirements before choosing a particular solution.

Generate possible tools

Generate up to four different ideas that could address your focus question. Think broadly before making a choice. A 'tool' can be a checklist, learning activity, feedback guide, protocol, visual aid or digital resource.

Select a tool to develop

Prefer an idea with strong exemplary value that is feasible to develop and test. Your selected tool will carry forward into Session 5.

Experiment with your prototype

Use the prototype in a relevant setting or collect feedback on a concept version. Focus on what users experience rather than assuming it works.

Evaluate and improve

Review user experiences, refine the tool and develop practical recommendations for its use and further development.

Reflect on innovating together

In a full Innovation Team this is a shared reflection on the process, learning and outcomes. You can capture individual reflections here and later discuss them with your team.

About this resource

This independent workshop adaptation supports the Innovation Team method by Anje Ros and Henderijn Heldens, developed through Fontys research. It is designed for a short EAPRIL introduction and for subsequent independent work; the original full method is collaborative and normally unfolds over multiple meetings.
